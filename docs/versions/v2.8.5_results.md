### 0.1 The registered axis, and the two within-version extensions

`changes.md` (131 lines, mtime 2026-08-10 02:37:27) registered one axis: the exponential hazard

```

```

against the deployed clipped ramp, at `ell ∈ {0.175, 0.25, 0.35}`, seed 42 only, described in the
document itself as "a screen, not a verdict-grade measurement" (`changes.md:79-80`).

- **`CLIP50`** — a budget-matched clip control. `launch__CLIP50.json` records its own role verbatim:
  `"CONTROL … not an arm; no hypothesis, no falsifier, no numeric prediction; changes.md NOT amended"`.

  whether it is bracketed from below. exp0.175's alpha switch surface `ell·ln2` is 0.1213 m, itself

## 4. Next axis

A suggestion, not a commitment.

1. **The collection-cadence axis, resolved rather than re-run.** §6.2 measures that net movement within
   a 100-step collection interval retains only a few percent of the summed per-step motion, below the
   random-walk reference, with no within-interval decay, while retention composed across intervals rises
   several-fold. `COMPRESS500` is that axis's first instantiation and returned NOT RESOLVED (§1.9), for
   two reasons that are both fixable: it was signalled 22 rounds short of its budget, so no matched
   terminal comparison exists; and at single seed the CIs overlap from round 300 onward. **A resolving
   measurement is a re-run to the full 500 rounds, and the decision on whether to make it multi-seed is
   the Researcher's.** Two of its columns are worth carrying into that design: its `saturation_rate` is
   the lowest of the seven conditions by a wide margin and its `timeout` is the highest.
2. **`buffer_cap` on `D_V`.** Never screened; only `policy_buffer_cap` has been (v2.3.0, 5000 → 2000).
   `D_V` never fills — `buffer_cap` 1e6 trajectories against roughly 100k accumulated over 500 rounds —
   so a step-1000 trajectory is sampled with the same probability at step 50000, and `V^{h,π}` is
   policy-dependent, so the value net is being asked to fit several policy generations at once.
3. **A forced-refill fraction between `legacy` and `continuing` collection.** The IC sampler is already
   uniform (`scene_init.py:380-388`); the `continuing` collector consumes that uniformity because
   refills happen only on R1/R2/R3, so coverage arrives through the buffer's memory rather than through
   fresh sampling. The middle is unexplored.
4. **`cps_v2` applicability on `quadrotor_3d` (C0).** An axis, not bookkeeping: resolving it re-scores
   every registered row. It has now closed two consecutive versions unresolved.
5. **Seed count, as a measurement-design item rather than an axis.** §1.4 measures that a single row's
   CI half-width at rounds ≥ 300 is 2.7×–4.1× the admissibility floor, so a difference of roughly 0.05
   is needed before two conditions' intervals come apart while the floor is passed at 0.0083. **Any
   difference this project cares about — the 0.0149 of §1.2 included — is inside that gap at one seed.**
   Recorded as a fact about the measurement; multi-seed escalation is Researcher-initiated only.
6. **The eval-cost repair, as engineering rather than as an axis.** §6.6 locates the whole of the
   contention factor in one function that is called per scene where its own file already calls the
   identical function batched, and the batched call is 336× cheaper. Making it batched is a local
   change in `evaluate.py` with a precedent in the same file whose own comment records that it was
   verified bit-identical. **It is not made in this version** and is the first item of the next.
   Arithmetic on the measured prices puts a contended in-loop eval at roughly 41 s against 673 s.
7. **Not recommended from this data:** any further label-side scalar or geometric lever.
   `rem:CG-limit` had already recorded that the label-side scalar path was exhausted and named the

   reach the deployed certificate. If the break is at `V̂` → action rather than at label → `V̂`, the
   lever is on the filter's feasible set, not on the hazard.

### 6.2 Within a collection interval, the overwhelming majority of gradient updates cancel

`R` = net displacement over summed per-step motion, six 100-step collection intervals, per probe:

| channel | probe P1 (IC-sampler probe) | probe P2 (on-policy probe) |
|---|---|---|
| policy | **0.080 – 0.140** | **0.051 – 0.068** |
| filtered action (`u_safe`) | **0.026 – 0.031** | **0.022 – 0.025** |
| value | **0.016 – 0.019** | **0.014 – 0.018** |

**`R` is at or below the 0.1 random-walk reference in all six intervals on both probes** — and below it
means the successive updates are **anti-correlated**: the movement is walked back, not merely
uncoordinated. The value net retains 1.4–1.9 % and the deployed action 2.2–3.1 % of the summed
per-step motion.

**There is no within-interval decay.** The 91–100 over 1–10 ratio, across all 36 cells (6 intervals ×
2 probes × 3 channels), spans **0.718 to 1.542** and is often above 1. The 100th update moves as far as
the first; it just does not move anywhere new.

**No boundary signal.** Post-collection (updates 1–10) over mid-interval (46–55), across the same 36
cells, spans **0.711 to 1.474** — 20 above 1 and 16 below.

**Composed across the six intervals, `R` rises several-fold:**

| channel | P1 | P2 |
|---|---|---|
| policy | **0.6249** | **0.5782** |
| filtered action | **0.3685** | **0.3736** |
| value | **0.2930** | **0.3340** |

**Stated plainly: almost all of the movement inside one collection interval is walked back; only the
part that comes from new data survives.** Progress comes from the sequence of collection rounds, not
from repeating updates on one of them.

reached, for `n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100}` updates. For the **value** net,
`d(n)/d(100)` reaches **0.86–1.00 by n = 10** on both probes, with several cells above 1.0 — the
trajectory doubles back. For the **policy** net the same curve sits on the pure-diffusion reference
`sqrt(n/100)` and reaches only **0.25–0.50** at n = 10. **That asymmetry is the registered risk the
compressed-update condition carried** (§1.9), and it was stated before that condition launched rather
than after.

### 6.4 The eval cost, and the two instrument findings it produced

Measured under controlled GPU residency and recorded because both findings outlive this version.

**`t_eval` was dead code.** `train.py` stamped `_t = time.time()` at the in-loop eval site but its
matching `timers.t_eval +=` sat **after a `break`, inside the halt branch**, and could never execute.
`t_eval` is therefore identically **0.0 on every row of every run in this version**, and every eval-cost
figure in the record had to be recovered from `wallclock_s` deltas. The other call site had the same
statement correctly placed, so the defect was specific to the site these runs use. **Repaired
2026-08-12**: the statement moved to the end of the `with eval_gate(...)` block, `verify.sh` green
(244 passed, 2 skipped), and every column of `eval_metrics.csv` identical pre versus post on both a
shipped smoke and a smoke-scale micro-run. **The repair takes effect only for processes started after
it; no historical `t_eval` is recovered and the seven conditions' rows stay 0.0.**

A structural fact found while verifying it, and worth keeping: **the shipped `--stage smoke` cannot
detect this defect**, because it sets `eval_cadence = n_steps` so its only eval fires on the terminal
step with no metrics row after it. Both the pre-change and post-change smokes read `t_eval = 0.0`.

**Where the eval's wall actually goes.** With no other process on the GPU, one 2000-scene in-loop eval
costs 16.4 s, of which the **200-step control loop is 2.705 s — 16.3 %** — and `_resolve_states`, the
per-scene LQR-baseline outcome resolution called once per scene, **12.878 s — 75.3 %**. Inside the
control loop, `filter_TOTAL` is 65.0 %, of which `_cbf_terms` including the grad-h autograd is 36.6 %
and the dual solve 25.3 %; the per-step host synchronization at `filter_hardnet.py:356` costs
**0.004 s over the whole eval, 0.02 % of the wall.** Stage totals close to 93.4 % of the loop and 99.4 %
of the eval, with the remainder itemized rather than left as a blob.

Three consequences, each measured rather than argued:
- **The branchless dual solve is bit-identical to the shipped form** — max abs difference exactly 0 on
  `u_safe`, the empty flag, the singular flag and the returned infeasible flag over 400,000 filter rows,
  with matching flag counts — and **its wall difference is not resolvable**: 20 interleaved repeats per
  form give medians 18.100 s and 18.076 s against within-form ranges of 3.652 s and 3.130 s. At the
  stage where the difference lives it is a few hundredths of a second per eval.
- **The shipped filter forward is NOT CUDA-graph capturable**, attempted here for the first time. Two
  blockers in sequence: `quadrotor_3d.py:94` copies an unpinned float64 CPU constant to the device on
  every `dynamics` call, and `filter_hardnet.py:356`'s host sync. With both removed capture succeeds,
  which establishes that **the grad-h autograd inside `_cbf_terms` is not itself a blocker** — the open
  question the docstring did not address. No captured-eval cost is reported, because none was built.
- **CPU pinning changes nothing.** The processes are unpinned across all 24 cores with a 24-thread
  PyTorch pool and draw **~1 core** in both training and eval windows. Constraining a fresh process to
  6 cores with `set_num_threads(6)`, and then to **1 core**, left `%CPU` at 100.0–100.1 in every repeat:
  the eval never wanted more than one core.

**What is not determined.** §E's whole-eval ratio between a 4-resident-process window and a
1-resident-process window is **33.6×**, reconstructed from disk, while training rows moved only 1.76×
over the same change. **Which part of the eval absorbs that factor is not established**: no stage
breakdown of a contended window exists, and seating it in the 200-step loop alone would require a 223×
loop factor. The uncontended `evaluate()` wall also drifted 16.4 → 19.3 s within a single window with
zero foreign GPU processes, and that drift's cause is undetermined; machine-wide CPU load was never
sampled.

### 6.6 Why `_resolve_states` makes 718,025 synchronizations, and why the repair already exists

Read from the source rather than inferred.

**`_resolve_states` (`evaluate.py:422-428`) is `resolve_outcome(step_outcomes(...))` and is called from
exactly one site** — `evaluate.py:219`, inside `if lqr_batch is not None`, **once per scene**.
`resolve_outcome` (`common/outcomes.py:155-200`) accepts masks of shape `[T, B]` and walks the time
axis; per step it performs four `torch.nonzero` calls inside `_record_events` (`:255`) and one
`bool(unresolved.any())` early-exit test (`:193`) — **five blocking synchronizations per step**. At
`B = 1` the early exit fires after roughly 72 of the 200 steps, giving about 360 synchronizations per
scene and **about 718,000 over 2000 scenes**, which is the measured 718,025.

**The policy path was already repaired, and the LQR path was left behind by that same repair.**
`evaluate.py:174-180` carries its own comment: *"v2.8.2 B1: outcome predicates computed ONCE per chunk
on the batched [T,B,.] states … ONE host transfer per chunk; masks are sliced per scene in the loop.
Bit-identical to the former per-scene calls (C1)."* That call resolves all 2000 scenes in **0.037 s**.
The LQR call four lines further down still runs per scene, at **12.878 s**. **The same function, the
same states shape, a factor of 336 apart, in one loop body.**

At `B = 2000` the time axis is walked once, so the synchronization count falls to roughly 1,000.
Arithmetic on the measured per-synchronization prices above — offered as arithmetic and not as a
prediction, since the repair has not been made:

| | measured now | after batching |
|---|---|---|
| residency 0, `evaluate()` | 18.4 s | ≈ 6.6 s |
| residency 3, `evaluate()` | 673.4 s | ≈ 41 s |

**Turning the baseline off in-loop is NOT the equivalent repair.** In-loop figures do consume
`lqr_states`: `train.py:2095` calls `write_trajectory_figures`, which builds each panel through
`_episode_control_spec` (`run_full.py:459-470`), whose line 469 sets
`nominal_states = episode.lqr_states`. What in-loop does **not** consume is the resolver's output —
`lqr_outcome` and `lqr_event_step` are read only at `run_full.py:220` (`filter_works_count`, and only
over `trajectories[:32]`) and in one verification script. **So the 0.554 s rollout is used and the
12.878 s resolution is not**, and batching keeps both callers whole while removing the cost.

**A protocol contradiction surfaced by the same read.** `04_eval` §3 specifies that for Joint Training
the dotted baseline in the trajectory figure is **the learned no-CBF rollout**. `run_full.py:469`
supplies **LQR**, on the in-loop and final figures alike. Carried as §12 P13.

**The repair is not made in this version.** It changes the eval path, the contention study was still
measuring on that path, and `06_workflow` §2.6 does not admit a code change into a close. It is the
