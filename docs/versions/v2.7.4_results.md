## 2. Verdicts

**H-B1 — CONFIRMED as a technical reading.** The filter cuts nominal collision 0.488 → 0.051. But the
hypothesis compared this against the M1 band, which was defined on nominal-only collision, a different
quantity; the two are not comparable. Strategist mis-registration, recorded rather than papered over.

**H-B2 — CONFIRMED.** JT cps 0.8508 CI-separated above pre-JT 0.6478 on the same pool and plant.
Collision falls 2.49x while reach rises to 0.9385 — not a trade. Starting from fully arbitrary attitude
reaches essentially the competence of v2.7.3's 120° cap.

**H-B3 — CONFIRMED, the version's actual claim.** Improvement CI-separated above zero in all three
strata, high-tilt floor +0.0697. Recovery from near-inverted starts was learned, not averaged over.
Largest in the middle stratum, smallest at the extreme.

**H-B-fallback (M7) — CONFIRMED on all three clauses; the adoption rule fires.** (i) Collision
CI-separated below, kstep upper 0.014 < none lower 0.015, gap 0.001 — marginal. (ii) Flip confinement
1.0. (iii) No paralysis: reach CI-separated above, timeout overlapping. kstep's benefit survives the
inverted empty-branch composition, weakened on every axis relative to v2.7.3 (2.2x against 3.2x
collision reduction, thinner separation). **The diagnostic program below shows the trial understated
its own result: kstep also removes 42% of the floor exits, which the M7 read did not examine.**

## 3. The finding that matters most: net, not universal

The paired flip analysis, run on identical initial conditions under both policies, exposes what
aggregate cps averages over: three forward flips against two reverse in the extreme stratum. Episode
1603 shows the mechanism cleanly — from 161° at 4.99 rad/s the JT arm drives tilt below 30° at 0.70 s
with distance-to-surface positive throughout, while pre-JT on the same start destabilizes and its
distance crosses zero. The honest statement is that joint training improves the distribution while
regressing a minority of starts. Only 1397 is a strict goal→collision reversal; 982 was already a
non-success. This is visible only because the comparison was paired.

## 4. Where the residual sits

**This section was rewritten four times as measurement invalidated each preceding account.** The
sequence is recorded because it is itself a result about method: no version of it was visible in
aggregate cps or in per-stratum deltas, and each correction came from tracing individual episodes.

### 4.1 The residual splits into two mechanisms that move oppositely with tilt

74 residual failures, exactly accounted: **41 cylinder collisions + 33 arena exits.** Every one of the
33 is a **floor** exit; **zero ceiling, zero lateral**.

| initial tilt | collision | oob (all floor) | combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| [0, 60) | 0.0230 (11/479) | 0.0125 (6) | 0.0355 |
| [60, 120) | 0.0227 (23/1012) | 0.0148 (15) | 0.0376 |
| [120, 180] | **0.0138** (7/509) | **0.0236** (12) | 0.0373 |

Collision falls with tilt while oob rises; the combined rate is nearly flat. **The high-tilt stratum
has the fewest cylinder collisions and the most floor exits** — 12 of its 19 failures are floor exits.
A vehicle busy righting itself does not fly into cylinders; it falls.

### 4.2 The floor is unprotected by construction, and gravity is the only uncontrolled bias

`h_star = phi(p_xy) + 0.3(v_xy · r_hat)` is invariant under vertical translation **and** under vertical
velocity shift: every term factors through the horizontal pair. The obstacle class is infinite vertical
cylinders, so this is not an oversight — altitude genuinely does not change cylinder distance. But the
arena floor is not a cylinder, and it appears nowhere in `h_star`. It is enforced only as a terminal
outcome. The code retrieve confirms the same absence on the objective side: **no term in
`losses.py` references altitude, vertical velocity, or the arena bound in any form.**

So the only unprotected direction coincides with the only direction carrying a persistent uncontrolled
bias. The 33/0/0 split is that coincidence made visible: laterally nothing pushes the vehicle out, and
vertically gravity pushes continuously while both the barrier and the objective are silent.

### 4.3 A plant limit the SO(3) axis runs into

Holding altitude requires the thrust axis to be within `arccos(1/TWR)` of vertical. At TWR = 2 that is
**60°**, and under uniform SO(3) the fraction of initial conditions above it is `(1 + 1/TWR)/2` = **75%**.
Three quarters of episodes begin unable to hold altitude at any thrust. Net vertical acceleration at
full thrust: 0.00 m/s² at 60°, −2.78 at 69°, −7.28 at 82.6°, −9.81 at 90°.

### 4.4 The failures were recoverable, repeatedly, and were not arrested

Tracing the 33 floor exits with a closed-form arrest margin
`m = (z - z_floor) - [|v_z|·t_rot + 0.5·g·t_rot² + v_z²/(2·a_up)]`, `t_rot = 2√(θ/α)`:

- **0 of 33 doomed at the initial condition.** Minimum `m(0)` over the 33 is +3.85 m; the arrest
  distance required at t=0 is a median 0.42 m against 6.75 m available. Initial margin does not
  predict floor exit — the floor-exit range [3.85, 11.05] lies entirely inside the non-failing range
  [3.13, 11.88].
- **0 of 33 committed before leaving the scene region.** At the moment they cross z = −4 they carry a
  median margin of **+2.97 m against the 0.96 m needed — 3.1x** — at median 1.80 s, v_z −2.36 m/s,
  tilt 69.0°.
- **Commitment comes at a median 4.10 s, tilt 82.6°**, with `h_star` median −0.394 (the cylinder hazard
  is clearly inactive) and `V_hat` median +0.071. The failure is not confounded with cylinder proximity.
- **33 of 33 had the goal ABOVE them** at basement entry, median +3.25 m, and **31 of 33 never closed
  that vertical gap afterwards** — the vehicle falls away from its objective for the whole transit.
- **217 of 1877 goal-reaching episodes also enter the basement** and recover, dipping a median 0.56 m.
  Entry is common and survivable; what separates the groups is what happens after.

### 4.5 What is actually happening during the fall

Comparing the learned policy's pre-filter command `u_nom` against the filter output `u_cmd` over the
basement transit (1625 steps across the 33; 14848 across the 217 controls):

| median [IQR] | 33 floor exits | 217 controls |
|---|---|---|
| total thrust, policy `u_nom` | 12.133 N (0.618 of max) | 15.703 N (0.800) |
| total thrust, filter `u_cmd` | **9.810 N (0.500)** | 14.979 N (0.764) |
| override ‖u_cmd − u_nom‖ | 5.607 | **0.000** |
| signed Δ thrust | −2.547 N | 0.000 |
| roll/pitch torque, nom → cmd | 0.8447 → 0.7943 N·m (−6%) | 0.1285 → 0.1284 |
| intervention fraction | **0.9032** | 0.1791 |
| **empty-branch fraction** | **0.5102** | 0.0548 |

Neither of the two candidate readings survives. **The filter is not causing the fall:** it removes only
2.5 N of 12.1 and passes 94% of the righting torque. **The policy is not idling:** it commands above
hover and 72% of the maximum roll/pitch torque.

The third state is what the numbers show. **On 51% of transit steps the feasible set is empty**, and
there `u_cmd` is produced by the `mode=none` empty-branch path, not by a constraint-satisfying
projection — its total thrust lands on exactly 9.810 and 4.905 N, which are 2x and 1x the per-rotor
maximum, i.e. box vertices. In the controls the filter is effectively inert over the same window
(override median exactly 0.000, empty 5.5%).

Two arithmetic facts complete the picture. At the 69° entry tilt, holding altitude would need 27.35 N
against a 19.62 N maximum — **even full thrust falls at 2.77 m/s²**, so the thrust deficit
(12.13 vs 19.62) is not the binding problem; the tilt is. And at the commanded 0.8447 N·m the vehicle
could rotate the 9° from 69° to the altitude-holding limit in **0.086 s** — yet it spends the 2.3 s
from basement entry to commitment without doing so. Since the reported torque is a magnitude, the
natural inference is that its direction is not consistently reducing tilt, which is what one expects
when half the steps have their action set by a fallback rule rather than by the policy. **The direction
was not measured and this inference is not established.**

### 4.6 The remedy was already measured and is not deployed

kstep k=5 reduces floor exits **0.0165 → 0.0095, 33 episodes to 19, a 42% reduction**, alongside the
54% collision reduction the M7 read reported. That is expected once the mechanism above is stated:
kstep is precisely the empty-branch rule, and this failure mode is in the empty branch on half its
steps. **Five diagnostic measurements converged on a remedy that was already in hand.**

It is not deployed. The v2.7.4 close attempted the `empty_fallback` default flip and HALTED: the config
block is global, so flipping it made the planar path exercise kstep, where `slice_scene` raised on a
non-dataclass scene. The default remains `none`. **The single highest-value open action in the lineage
is the per-system plumbing fix that unblocks a change whose adoption rule has already fired.**

## 5. Findings not registered in advance

**V_hat's inflation is not carried by the velocity term, and the earlier evidence for that was the
wrong statistic.** The contour figures showed the minimum of `V_hat` rising more on the velocity slices
(+0.318 and +0.281 at |v|=1.5 against +0.177 at v=0), and that was cited in support. Measuring the
**mean** over a fixed 41×41 grid at four radial speeds reverses it: the rise is largest at v = 0
(+0.2144) and shrinks monotonically to +0.1078 at |v|=1.5, and the outside-cylinder subset gives the
same ordering. Against the M3 initialization the JT stage **raises** `V_hat` by +0.1915 at v = 0 and
**lowers** it by −0.0629 at |v| = 1.5 — a sign change across the speed range. A single order statistic
per slice gave the opposite answer to the mean; that is the lesson, not the number.

**A candidate competence diagnostic.** The speed sensitivity `dV_hat/d|v_radial|`, OLS over the four
measured speeds on the fixed grid:

| checkpoint | slope | as a multiple of the hazard coefficient c = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|
| M3 (value only) | +0.3596 | 1.20 |
| JT step 1500 | +0.2603 | 0.87 |
| JT step 50000 | +0.1889 | **0.63** |

Robust on the outside-cylinder subset (0.4259 → 0.2199, 48% drop) and monotone M3 > early > final in
both. Since `V ≥ h_star` with equality where the discounted supremum is attained at t = 0, and
`dh_star/dv_radial = c` exactly, a slope near c indicates a value dominated by the instantaneous
hazard, and a slope below c indicates that the policy converts incoming radial speed into less future
hazard. The full 34-checkpoint series is non-monotone — it rises to 0.3266 at step 7500 before falling,
against checkpoint-to-checkpoint noise of 0.005–0.010 — so the registered monotonicity prediction was
**falsified**; over the post-transient window 7500 → 50000 the fall is consistent and plateaus at step
40500, close to the selected checkpoint at 39000.

**The learned certificate's authority is 98.4% torque.** Decomposing `L_g V_hat` through the fixed
mixer and weighting each channel by its available range: median f_thr 0.4365 against tau_x 12.1944,
tau_y 10.4797, tau_z 0.8110 — **thrust share 0.0158**, at most 0.1507 in any 10° tilt bin. This is
notable because `h_star` itself has **zero** torque authority: `dh_star/dR = dh_star/dω = 0`, so
`L_g h_star` lives entirely in the thrust channel. The relative-degree-1 collapse does not merely
restore authority, it relocates it. The ratio decomposes as a physical leverage factor of 12.0
(J⁻¹ = 100 against 1/m = 1, after channel-range normalization) times an implied
|∇_ω V| / |∇_v V| ≈ 2.3. The gate's own sampling under-tests this: 3441 of 4372 boundary states sit at
tilt [0,10) because the sampler rolls the cascaded-PD nominal, which rights quickly, so the high-tilt
regime where the failures live is represented by tens of states.

**Chattering correlates with path length and duration** (r = 0.475 against path, 0.578 against step
count, 0.659 for saturation against path), correlation not causation, with a counterexample (episode
43). Realized path length is **flat** across training (1.29–1.41 after the early transient), so the
detours are a level property, not something that accumulates.

**Realized speed rises over training** (mean |v_xy| 1.2518 → 1.5443, +23%; p90 nearly flat at
2.411 → 2.434 after step 4500, so the upper tail is bounded while the mean rises).

**Empty-branch composition inverted** relative to v2.7.3: 2.85% rate with a 7.45% singular fraction,
against v2.7.3's nearly all-singular population. This is what makes the M7 result informative — kstep
was adopted on an all-singular composition and its benefit survives the inversion, weakened.

**Per-episode outcomes can depend on batch composition.** Episodes 1241 and 1397 changed outcome
between a subset re-roll and the full-pool re-roll, then matched canonical on the full pool. Not eval
non-determinism (canonical reproduces bit-identically) but reduction-order sensitivity near the
decision boundary — a caution against loading a single episode with evidential weight.

**The situational objective is planar by construction.** The approach/brake term (`w_appr`,
`tau_brake`) is obstacle-relative and **purely horizontal** — its position input is sliced to the
obstacle-plane and its velocity input is `horizontal_velocity`, documented as xy-only because cylinders
are infinite. The settling, goal-distance and terminal terms **are** isotropic, so vertical goal error
does enter at full weight. v2.6.2 built these terms gated on `system is quadrotor_planar`; v2.7.2
replaced that with a capability test (`hasattr(system, "horizontal_velocity")`), which is what causes
quadrotor_3d to inherit them. The only 3-D-specific adjustment on record narrows the approach term to
xy rather than retuning it, and no per-system weight override exists.

## 6. What is not established

**The joint-versus-alternating comparison does not exist.** No value/policy alternating arm was run
anywhere in the v2.7.x lineage. Every comparison is pre-JT (untrained policy) against JT, which
establishes that joint training beats doing nothing — not the claim the framework rests on. Until an
alternating arm from the same M3 value is run, the central methodological question is open. This is the
most consequential gap in the lineage. Note that the fallback mode is no longer a neutral choice for
that experiment: with `mode=none`, half the steps of the failure mode have their action set by a
fallback rule rather than by the policy, which is not a clean setting in which to compare gradient paths.

**Whether the empty branch kills the BPTT gradient.** If the `mode=none` output is a box vertex
independent of `u_nom`, then `du_cmd/du_nom = 0` there and those steps contribute no policy gradient —
a degeneracy distinct from `L_g V_hat = 0`, and not covered by the existing joint-training gradient
argument. The observed vertex-valued outputs are consistent with this but the code path was not read.

**The torque direction during the fall.** The measurement reports magnitude only. Whether the commanded
torque consistently reduces tilt is the natural next question and is unanswered.

Single seed, 42. No multi-seed claim made or registered. This bears especially on M7's marginal
collision separation (gap 0.001).

**EXPLORATORY status.** M1 used a nominal-only collision band insensitive on the attitude axis, and the
selected item was nearest the band, not inside it.

**Sim-to-real unaddressed.** Motor lag, control-rate mismatch, estimation noise and perception are all
absent, and no hardware or PX4 sim-in-the-loop validation exists. The commanded control is non-smooth
by construction. The adopted kstep fallback costs 30.6x wall clock, which is a deployment-feasibility
question, not a result here.

## 7. Calibration, and a lesson that recurred three times

v2.7.3 M1 registered `frac>=1` in a band the scene acceptance rule makes unreachable. v2.7.4 M1
registered a nominal-only collision band on an axis where nominal collision is geometry-saturated.
Both were rescued by measuring the property directly instead of through the proxy.

The third instance is in this diagnostic program and was the Strategist's own. G3 registered "the
majority of the arrest margin is lost above the scene-region floor" as the discriminator between a
geometric and a barrier-side cause. Under a null model in which the vehicle simply descends, that
fraction is `(z_0 + 4)/(z_0 + 7)` — **a function of the starting altitude alone**. Inverting the
observed median 0.5392 and IQR [0.387, 0.682] gives implied start altitudes of −0.49 [−2.11, +2.43],
against the sampler's U(−4, 4) with median 0 and IQR [−2, +2]. The metric reproduced the start-altitude
distribution and could not have discriminated anything. The registered criterion returned CONFIRMED
while the count the same prompt called "the cleanest evidence" returned 0 of 33 the other way.

The rule already in `00_constitution` §4 — that a calibration proxy is validated per axis before its
band is trusted — extends to diagnostic metrics, and the check is cheap: state the null model under
which the metric is uninformative, and confirm the data can distinguish it.
