## 2. Versus motivation

`changes.md` §2 stated two things: that the band term would reduce band collisions, and that the anticipation
carrying it would come from the value rather than from the hazard's lead term.

**Band collisions fall, CI-separated, on every pool measured.** From
`data/previous_runs/v2.7.6/stage2_eval/collision_split/`, banded scoring, counts out of 2000:

| checkpoint | pool | collision total | cylinder | band | floor / ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JT 42000 | band-feasible (`185659a7`) | 0.0235 [0.0175, 0.0305] | 39 | 8 | 8 / 0 |
| v2.7.4 | band-feasible (`185659a7`) | 0.1100 [0.0965, 0.1235] | 35 | 185 | 177 / 8 |
| JT 42000 | full-range (`db0b9eb5`) | 0.0760 [0.0655, 0.0865] | 53 | 99 | 98 / 1 |
| v2.7.4 | full-range (`db0b9eb5`) | 0.1575 [0.1425, 0.1725] | 29 | 286 | 276 / 10 |

Band collisions go 185 → 8 and 286 → 99. The surface is 96–99% floor in every arm, as the vertical asymmetry
of the hazard predicts. The split also carries the cost: cylinder collisions rise, 35 → 39 on band-feasible and
29 → 53 on full-range. The vertical branch competes for the same filter row as the horizontal ones, and it wins
that competition most often exactly where the vertical risk is real.

**The anticipation is in the value, and it moved the channel it was predicted to move.** The registered
informative form of prediction 2 — that the thrust-channel share of the certificate's authority exceeds the
band-blind JT reference 0.0735 — is confirmed at 0.20357 overall and 0.71127 on the band boundary, n = 41147
(`data/previous_runs/v2.7.6/stage2_eval/pred2prime_thrust_share.json`). Before this version the learned certificate's
authority was almost entirely torque; on the band boundary it is now predominantly thrust. That is the channel
the vertical branch's $L_g$ is supported on, so the mechanism is confirmed and not merely the outcome.

The level-set readout says the same thing in position space. At the selected checkpoint, recomputed from
`…/checkpoints/step_042000.pt`, grid spacing 0.1333 m (figure `stage2_eval/yz_headline_step42000.png`):

| row (readout $p_y$) | hover floor | hover ceiling | tilt-120 floor | tilt-120 ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| row0 (2.667) | +0.2242 | −0.0969 | +0.7846 | +0.5124 |
| row1 (3.600) | +2.1437 | −2.4072 | +1.0275 | −1.1448 |

Both columns lie inside the sampled support. The tilt-120 floor offsets, +0.78 and +1.03, are the quantity
that matters: past the altitude-holding limit no lead coefficient can make the hazard self-consistent, so the
value must turn positive well above the hazard's own zero line, and it does. The hover floor offsets are near
zero at row0 as expected and large at row1, where the policy applies almost no upward thrust across the
descent — a reading about the policy's altitude effort, not about the value. The **ceiling** offsets are
negative in both rows: on that surface the value turns positive later than the hazard asks. Ceiling collisions
are 0 and 1 out of 2000, so this costs nothing measured here, and it is recorded rather than explained.

**What made all of it possible was not the hazard term.** The band branch was added first under the dim-32
observation, which carried only goal-relative position; the vertical margin $|p_z| - z_{\lim}$ is a function of
absolute altitude, so states with different band hazard mapped to one observation and the value could not
represent its own target. A full JT run was spent before that was found. The superseded arm reads banded
full-range −0.1758 (`docs/ledger.md` row 14), against 0.42267 for the same stage after the observation was
extended to dim 34 (`stage2_eval/v276_oc_a_dim34_fullrange.json`). The one committed non-default code change
this version leaves behind is `env.obs_band_z = True`; the band-hazard and band-collision flags default off and
are set per run.

## 3. Improvement verdict

**Improved, CI-separated, single seed — under this version's scoring, at matched deploy settings.**

On the canonical pool under banded scoring with the same fallback on both arms, JT 42000 reads
0.80508 [0.7744, 0.8349] against v2.7.4's 0.54452 [0.4991, 0.5884]. The intervals do not overlap. At
`fallback = none` the same comparison reads 0.69287 [0.6559, 0.7314] against 0.45956 [0.4107, 0.5067], also
separated. The margin is what the band collisions bought.

**Under legacy scoring the two are not separated in either direction on the canonical pool**: 0.80288
[0.7730, 0.8288] against 0.84859 [0.8252, 0.8708], overlapping by 0.0036 at `none`; 0.90783 [0.8899, 0.9253]
against 0.89672 [0.8780, 0.9134] at `kstep`, also overlapping. Read plainly: the vertical branch costs nothing
detectable on the metric that ignores the surface it defends, and wins large on the metric that counts it.

**One registered prediction was falsified and the falsification stands.** Prediction 3 stated that legacy cps
on the retained full-range pool would not regress CI-separated against v2.7.4. It did: 0.7986 [0.770, 0.826]
against 0.8580 [0.835, 0.881] (`stage2_eval/jt42000_fullrange.json`, `stage2_eval/v274_fullrange.json`). The
canonical pool does not reproduce that separation, but the prediction named the full-range pool, so it is
scored on the pool it named. The regression is deploy-contingent — it disappears under the fallback, where
full-range legacy reads 0.9085 [0.891, 0.924] against 0.8990 [0.880, 0.915]
(`fallback_trial/m7_fallback_trial.json`) — but that is a separate measurement, not a re-scoring of the
prediction.

**The comparison baseline is not a secured one.** `data/secured_data/` holds nothing newer than v2.7.2;
v2.7.4, v2.7.5, and v2.7.6 are absent, and the parent checkpoint `244f4f83` is referenced only by run
directory. The verdict above is therefore rendered against a measured parent rather than against a secured
baseline, and the `04_eval` §5 chain is broken at v2.7.3 until those promotions happen.

**Verification state.** `scripts/verify.sh` 183 passed / 2 skipped / 0 failed. Both blocking pool SHAs
(`0ef3751b`, `db0b9eb5`) bit-identical throughout. The M8 vectorization gate reproduced the M7 arm with 0
mismatches out of 2000 at identical cps. No blocking gate was skipped.

**Registered predictions that scored without informing.** Three of this version's registrations were
satisfied or saturated before the run they were meant to test: prediction 1 (banded collision falls against
v2.7.4) was already true at the pre-JT stage; prediction 2 used a threshold from a different checkpoint class;
and the informative-form prediction was placed on the band-feasible pool, where band collisions are near zero
by construction and the axis cannot move. Each is scored as registered and marked uninformative. The remedy is
in `06_workflow` §2.4 — a prediction is registered against a measurement that can resolve it — and not in any
re-fitting of these three.

## 4. Next axis

A suggestion, not a commitment.

**The empty branch is now the largest identified defect, and it is one the project's central claim runs
through.** Infeasibility on the canonical pool is 0.1421 at `none` against 0.1000 under the fallback, and a
crude k-step lookahead over the empty rows is worth roughly 0.11 banded cps on full-range
(`stage2_eval/m8_sweep.json`: 0.6874 at `none` against 0.8027 at `p1_k3`). The sweep also showed that almost
all of that comes from looking ahead at all: a second phase buys −0.0013 and horizons past k = 3 lose ground.
Meanwhile the deployed enumerator is exhaustive only for two-dimensional action spaces (`02_control` §6.1) and
this system runs at four, so the filter is simultaneously approximate on non-empty rows and patched on empty
ones, and the policy receives no gradient wherever a box corner wins. Replacing the enumeration with a
dimension-general exact projection is a standing prerequisite that this system has never satisfied.

**The central claim remains untested.** Both checkpoints compared here are jointly trained; nothing in this
version separates joint training from alternating training. That comparison should be run under a filter whose
gradient does not vanish on a large fraction of steps, which is the same prerequisite as above and is the
reason to take it first.

**Smaller items the data names.** The kstep lookahead is CPU-resident and un-vectorized at deployment, so its
measured per-step latency is the loading path's cost rather than the algorithm's; the deployed `p1_k3` setting
has no canonical-pool measurement; and the secured-data chain needs v2.7.4 onward promoted before any further
verdict can cite a secured baseline.
