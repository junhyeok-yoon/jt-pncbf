## 4. The evaluation repair

### 4.1 What was wrong

`_resolve_states` (`src/eval/evaluate.py`) resolves the LQR baseline's outcome and was called **once
per scene**, inside the per-scene loop, while the policy path four lines above already resolved all
2000 scenes in one batched call. The same function, the same states shape, in one loop body.

`resolve_outcome` accepts masks of shape `[T, B]` and walks the time axis; per step it makes four
`torch.nonzero` calls and one early-exit test — five blocking synchronizations per step. At `B = 1`
the early exit fires partway through, giving a few hundred synchronizations per scene and
**722 025 over 2000 scenes**, against the 200-step control loop's 800.

### 4.2 The repair and its acceptance test

The resolution was hoisted out of the per-scene loop and performed once per chunk on the batched
states, and the loop slices the result.

**H1's falsifier is any byte difference in either evaluation output.** The fact-gather re-verified it
by direct byte comparison of `eval_metrics.csv` and `eval_episodes.csv` across seven independent
scorings of the same checkpoint on the same pool — `before` against `after`, `before_count`,
`after_count`, and three residency repeats.

| comparison | `eval_metrics.csv` | `eval_episodes.csv` |
|---|---|---|
| all six pairings against `before` | **IDENTICAL** | **IDENTICAL** |

The score row is the same in all seven: `cps 0.8132833844311224`, reach 0.9325, collision 0.0335,
infeasibility 0.11738871856292524, 2000 episode rows. **H1 passes.**

## 5. The buffer investigation

Four build-logs. This is the largest body of work in the version and none of it was a registered
axis; it is recorded here because the build-logs are untracked.

### 5.1 `buffer_cap` changes composition, not repetition

Let `d` be the transitions drawn per round (value updates × batch) and `n` the transitions entering
per round. The expected number of times a transition is drawn over its life in the buffer is
`E = d/n`, and the cap **cancels**: a larger cap makes any one draw less likely and the residency
proportionally longer. Measured: **`E = 12.288`, identical across every finite cap.**

**So `buffer_cap` is a purely distributional lever.** What it changes is which rounds are resident at
any moment — 10.8, 26.9 and 53.8 rounds for caps 2 000, 5 000 and 10 000, against an unbounded cap
that does not bind inside a 1000-round run.

For the unbounded cap the per-transition draw count is not uniform in time: a transition entering at
round `r₀` of a run of length `R` is drawn `12.288·ln(R/r₀)` times, so the earliest data is drawn
**84.9** times and the latest **1.5**. The unbounded buffer is dominated by its own oldest, worst
data.

### 5.2 What the buffer holds, and what it does not

- **`D_V` is not in the checkpoint schema.** The checkpoint's top level carries eleven keys —
  version, framework, step, best cps and step, the two value states, the policy state, two optimizer
  states, and the config. **The buffer is not recoverable from any checkpoint**, which closes the
  whole "reconstruct the historical buffer" line of enquiry by a single negative.
- **No buffer-occupancy column exists** in `metrics.csv`; none of its 88 columns reports it.
- **The buffer's `h` distribution is NOT DETERMINABLE from disk.** Three remedies were named and none
  was executed in this version.
- **`v_label_episodes` is carried in the config with no read site anywhere under `src/`** — a dead key.

### 5.3 The two candidate mechanisms, separated

Two shapes could explain why a larger buffer scores higher: a **frozen-label** effect, where older
trajectories carry higher labels and dropping them makes the certificate less conservative; and a
**state-coverage** effect, where the small buffer simply never trains near the boundary.

Both value nets were evaluated on **one common set of 21 505 states**, stratified by horizontal
clearance, at the nearest matched round.

| clearance | small-buffer IQR ÷ large-buffer IQR |
|---|---|
| boundary, 0.05–0.20 m | **1.32 – 1.34** |
| far, > 0.35 m | **0.92 – 0.97** |

**The ratio crosses 1 at the boundary and inverts beyond it.** The small buffer's values are *more*
dispersed exactly where the filter operates and *less* dispersed where it does not.

**Shape 1 is contradicted, not merely unsupported** — the sign of the median difference is opposite
to what a frozen-label bias predicts. **Shape 2 is supported.** Two stated limits: the magnitude is
about 1.3×, not an order of magnitude; and at one seed there is **no null band**, so whether it
survives a seed replicate is NOT DETERMINED. A two-seed replicate of the measurement itself was
stable to ≤ 0.01 in every median and ≤ 0.04 in every ratio.

### 5.4 Where the certificate is least reliable

Per-stratum signed value error, both checkpoints: **the sign disagreement peaks in the
(0.05, 0.10] m clearance stratum — 0.2486 and 0.2922** — and falls monotonically with clearance. The
disagreement is **dispersion, not bias**.

**That stratum is where the filter operates and where its sign is least reliable.** And it is thin:
clearance ≤ 0.10 m is **1.060 % of buffer transitions = 43.4 rows of a uniform 4096-row minibatch**,
of which the peak-disagreement stratum contributes **30.8 rows**.

### 5.5 The stationary detector is cutting arrivals, not stalls

The collector refills a row on one of three conditions — goal held, stationary, or timeout. Over 14
instrumented rounds the stationary condition fires **749 times against the goal condition's 542**.

**Of those 749 closures, 99.20 % end inside the goal radius and 99.60 % at or below goal speed.**
Only **3 of 749 (0.40 %)** end more than a metre from the goal. The mechanism is the **angular
settling gate, satisfied on only 4.7 %**: the vehicle arrives, holds position, and never settles its
attitude, so the goal condition cannot claim the episode and the stationary condition takes it.

**Consequence for the buffer.** The stationary-closed segments hold **28.0 % of buffer transitions**,
of which **16.99 % of the whole buffer is stationary tail** — against a construction floor of 10.7 %
from the 20-step detection window alone, so the measured figure is 1.6× the floor. 620 of 749
segments carry a run of at least 20 stationary steps and 252 are below threshold over their entire
length.

**The collector's stationary test and the evaluator's `stuck` are not the same test** — they differ
in all five of window (20 vs 60 steps), threshold (0.05 vs 0.10 m), statistic, comparison
strictness, and definedness. On the instrumented dump the contingency is 0 both / 0 evaluator-only /
**764 collector-only** / 56 853 neither. **The evaluator's predicate has zero positives on this
data**, so the reverse question is undecidable — an explicit null.

## 6. The pool audit

A read-only forensic audit of how the three pools of record were built. Nothing was built,
regenerated or modified. **Every SHA of the three pools recomputed from disk matches its manifest.**

### 6.1 Five ways the pools do not meet `04_eval`'s own requirements

1. **The altitude law.** `04_eval` §6.1 requires the eval pool to be the **recoverable restriction**
   on the vertical axis, drawn by redrawing z conditioned on initial tilt. That machinery exists in
   the sampler. **All four pools were drawn under the unrestricted branch instead**, established from
   the stored data by a support test that eliminates every other branch. The pools carry starts
   within 0.005 m of the band surface.
2. **The screen is unregistered.** The `coupled_bound_only` screen that produced the registered pool
   of record appears **nowhere in `docs/protocol/`** — searches for it, for `pool_succession`, for
   the pool stems and for the seeds all return NOT FOUND. Its governing authority is a Researcher
   ruling inside a version document at a gitignored path.
3. **The registered pools are not the pools in use.** `04_eval` §6.2 registers an in-loop pool at
   n 500 seed 12345 and a full pool at n 2000 seed 23456; the pools in use are n 2000 at seeds
   145678 and 823456.
4. **The headline pool cannot be regenerated.** `04_eval` §6.2 requires a byte-identical rebuild from
   the same seed and sampler params. The `full` pool is an independent draw **whose producing
   invocation is nowhere in the tree**, and the shipped CLI is positively ruled out because it loads
   only the base config while the manifest snapshot contains a key that exists only in the
   experiment config.
5. **The payload shape.** `04_eval` pins the `.pkl` to a dict with a fixed key order. **`fullcb`'s
   `.pkl` is a dataclass instance**; the other four pools examined are the pinned dict. The loader
   tolerates both, so nothing breaks on read.

**And the protocol contradicts itself on training's vertical draw.** `04_eval` §6.1 describes
training as drawing z over the full arena band including unrecoverable low starts; `03_train` §1.5
describes it as clearing the band by the horizontal clearance. The code follows `03_train`. Whether
the train/eval asymmetry is sanctioned is **NOT STATED** in `docs/protocol/` and therefore **NOT
DETERMINED**.

### 6.2 A defect the manifests cannot show

**The sampler's angular-rate maximum exceeds the plant's per-component clamp**, so `wrap_state` cuts
the stored value on first read. Measured: **109 of 2000** scenes in `full` and `fullcb`, and **147 of
2000** in `inloopv2`, store an initial condition that is not the state the rollout begins from.

**No pool manifest records this, and none can**, because the manifest's sampler snapshot omits the
system's own IC block, the band-hazard block, `dt` and `max_steps`. **No manifest records which
altitude branch produced its z**, which is why §6.1 item 1 had to be established from the data.

### 6.3 What the screen actually does, and what it does not claim

The coupled bound reads position, velocity, tilt angle and the roll/pitch rate — **attitude enters,
and it is the load-bearing input** — and integrates a closed-form reachable envelope over a
4052-point time grid to 10 s, scanned over a 16-point frontier. It is **neither a one-step test nor a
recursive multi-step propagation**: it is a single-time containment test over that grid.

It removed 33 scenes from the predecessor pool — **32 floor-bound and 1 ceiling-bound, zero
obstacle-bound** — and every removal's recorded margin equals the manifest's to all recorded digits.
Every removal's horizontal slack is strictly positive. The soundness post-condition holds: no flagged
scene was survived by the prior leader. Its own manifest states the limit: it excludes scenes
certified impossible by a plant-only bound and is **not a viability oracle**.

**The top-up has a shape the audit records rather than defends.** 33 replacements were drawn under a
different seed with **only** the coupled bound applied — no band clearance, no tilt cap, no floor
screen — and one admitted replacement sits 0.0988 m from the ceiling with a coupled margin equal to
that number exactly. The 1967 inherited scenes match the predecessor scene-for-scene with **0
mismatches across all seven stored arrays**, the replacements are contiguous at the end, and the
recomputed tilts match the recorded ones as a sorted multiset. `fullcb`'s manifest copies its
predecessor's sampler snapshot verbatim, so that block documents the ancestor's build and not the
replacement draw.
