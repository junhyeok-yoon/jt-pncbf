# v2.1.0 Results

Diagnosis-only version, run entirely on the secured v2.0.1 seed-42 checkpoint
(read-only; no training, no weight changes). Two pieces of work: an eval-only
lookahead-alpha filter ablation, and a failure diagnosis (dissection + signal pilot).
SOTA is unchanged.

---

## 1. Lookahead-alpha filter — rejected

### 1.1 What was tested

An eval-only, deploy-time modification that inflates the CBF class-K gain alpha when a
short detached lookahead rollout predicts the state becoming less safe:

    alpha_t = alpha_base(h_t) * (1 + beta * ReLU((h_peak_N - h_t) / delta))

h_peak_N is the maximum h over an N-step no-grad rollout of policy + HardNet from x_t.
The lookahead-internal HardNet uses alpha_base; only the outer executed projection uses
alpha_t. Defaults beta=0, N=0 reproduce v2.0.1 unchanged.

### 1.2 Fidelity checks (so the negative result is real signal)

- L0 (N=0, beta=0) reproduced the secured v2.0.1 seed-42 row exactly:
  cps 0.8852928467542948, collision 0.006, reach 0.958, saturation 0.12155.
- `scripts/verify.sh`: 35 passed, 1 warning.
- Read-only implementation audit (`docs/versions/v2.1.0/lookahead_impl_audit.md`):
  term-by-term match to the formula; multiplier always >= 1 (inflation only); inner
  rollout uses alpha_base, only the outer projection uses alpha_t; h_peak includes k=0;
  rollout detached; deployed mean-ensemble h. No sign flip, delta misuse, inner/outer
  swap, or target/deployed confusion. Verdict: faithful implementation of a
  mis-directed formulation.

### 1.3 Ablation grid (6 arms x 3 seeds, eval-only)

| arm | N | beta | mean cps | mean collision | vs L0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L0 | 0 | — | 0.8777 | 0.0053 | baseline |
| L1 | 5 | 2.0 | 0.8562 | 0.0113 | -0.0215 |
| L2 | 15 | 2.0 | 0.8564 | 0.0120 | -0.0213 |
| L3 | 30 | 2.0 | 0.8492 | 0.0133 | -0.0285 |
| L4 | 15 | 0.5 | 0.8592 | 0.0100 | -0.0185 |
| L5 | 15 | 5.0 | 0.8418 | 0.0160 | -0.0359 |

All six arms x three seeds (42/12345/99) regressed cps and raised collision relative to
L0. The grid is a clean dose-response in both directions. At fixed N=15, increasing the
inflation strength beta monotonically worsens cps (L4 beta=0.5 0.8592, L2 beta=2.0 0.8564,
L5 beta=5.0 0.8418) and raises collision (0.0100, 0.0120, 0.0160). At fixed beta=2.0,
increasing the horizon N worsens cps (L1 0.8562, L2 0.8564, L3 0.8492). The monotone
degradation with inflation magnitude is the causal confirmation of the §1.4 mechanism: a
stronger or more frequently triggered alpha inflation loosens the safe-region barrier more,
and collides more.

### 1.4 Mechanism

HardNet half-space `{u : A*u <= b}`, `A = L_g h`, `b = -L_f h - alpha*h`, with h <= 0
safe. For a safe state approaching an obstacle (h < 0):

    d b / d alpha = -h > 0

Increasing alpha increases b, which enlarges the admissible set — the filter becomes
more permissive and allows faster approach. The lookahead fires while the state is still
safe (h < 0), so it loosens the barrier exactly where it intends to tighten it. In the
unsafe region (h > 0) inflation would be conservative, but the lookahead does not act
there.

A 7-state read-only probe (h in [-0.30, -0.02], positive closing speed, L2 settings)
confirmed this at the state level: in every state where the projected action changed, it
moved toward the obstacle relative to base-alpha (e.g. +0.212, +0.020); none moved away.
States with the largest inflation (~12x) were unchanged because their constraint was not
binding — the degradation came from moderate inflation on binding constraints.

### 1.5 Verdict

The hypothesis "alpha inflation reduces collision" is falsified, with the mechanism
identified analytically (db/dalpha = -h > 0 in the safe region), confirmed numerically at
the state level (§1.4), and confirmed causally by the completed dose-response: degradation
scales monotonically with inflation magnitude (§1.3). The lookahead quantity h_peak_N - h_t
is itself a valid forward-risk quantity; the failure is in the lever it was attached to,
not in the quantity.

## 2. Failure dissection — methodology and results

### 2.1 Setup

Large diagnostic rollout on the v2.0.1 seed-42 best checkpoint, N=2500, seed 45678
(`data/diagnostics/v2.0.1__20260529-171057__failure_dissect_n2500_seed45678_20260529-194915/`).
Per-step NPZ traces saved for all 131 failures and 120 matched goal controls.

Outcome counts: goal 2342, collision 26, stuck 105, timeout 27, oob 0. Stuck and timeout
are higher than in the N=500 scoring pool because this is a larger, harder draw made to
characterize failures, not to score cps. Target-net h is not present in the NPZ.

### 2.2 Collision

Mechanism labeling (`jt_failure_dissect.py`): box-infeasible if the last-10-step window
has mean half-space/box emptiness above 0.3, else small ||L_g h||, false-safe brake
margin, high projection/saturation, or committed high-speed approach. Result: all 26
collisions share the box-infeasible mechanism — the action-set intersection becomes
empty before impact.

Layer attribution (`jt_failure_layer_attribution.py`): Top-K=5 obstacle membership from
the saved layout, culprit "observed" if in Top-K for >= 80% of the window; scenes flagged
large-detour by no finite A* path, safe-path ratio >= 1.30, initial turn >= 60 deg, or
deeply blocked clearance < -0.30 with ratio >= 1.20. Collisions assigned to
observation-limit (not observed), dynamics-doomed-needs-planning (large detour), or
fixable-upstream-policy.

Result: 0/26 observation-limit, 2/26 dynamics/planning, 24/26 (92.3%)
fixable-upstream-policy. The culprit obstacle is visible in all 26 cases, so collisions
are not a perception failure — they are a reactive single-step filter limit.

Lead time (collision is foreshadowed, not sudden):
- Box/half-space first empty: mean 29.0, median 23.5, range 5-71 steps before impact.
- Brake margin first negative: mean 11.0, median 6.5, range 0-44 steps before impact.

### 2.3 Stuck

Mechanism classification over a 60-step window: policy stagnation, near-obstacle
projection/saturation trap (projection > 1.0, saturation > 0.5, clearance min < 0.35),
overconservative trap, oscillatory chattering (sign-change rate > 0.35), positive-h
blocking. Layer pass assigns planning-limit-geometric-trap for large event-to-goal detour
with high projection/saturation, else fixable-stuck-chatter.

Result: 95/105 (90.5%) planning-limit-geometric-trap, 10/105 fixable-stuck-chatter.

Stuck-window metrics show a trapped, not idle, controller: net displacement 0.053 m over
a 0.382 m path (path/net ratio 10.2), action sign-change rate 0.685, mean ||u_nom|| 1.534,
||u_safe|| 2.288, projection norm 2.430, saturation 0.867. The stall is at near-obstacle
margins, not far-field h>0 — a reactive controller oscillating inside a local-minimum
geometry it cannot escape.

### 2.4 Reactive ceiling

The two modes together give an estimated reactive cps ceiling of about 0.91, leaving
roughly +0.025 above v2.0.1 SOTA. The lookahead-alpha axis attempted to recover the
collision-side headroom with a reactive lever and failed.

## 3. Failure-signal pilot — is failure predictable?

### 3.1 Setup and caveat

`docs/versions/v2.1.0/failure_signal_pilot.md`. Retrospective analysis over the saved NPZ
only — no new rollout, no detector.

**What is scored.** Each example is a single timestep. A positive is a timestep exactly K
steps before a failure event (event_action_step - K); a negative is a goal-control
timestep at a matched within-episode index. Collision-vs-goal and stuck-vs-goal are
separate two-class problems sharing the 120 goal controls as the negative class. K is
swept over {1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30}. The negatives are the matched goal controls, not all
non-failure timesteps, which makes the comparison easier than deployment — so the results
below are existence checks for separable signals, not operational detector metrics.

**The score s_i of a timestep.** Each timestep i is assigned one scalar score s_i, by
convention larger = more failure-like, in one of two ways:

- *Single-signal.* s_i = sign * x_i, where x_i is one raw per-step signal at timestep i
  and sign is fixed in advance: +1 if higher values are failure-like (h, ||L_g h||,
  box-emptiness, saturation, projection, closing speed), -1 otherwise (clearance, brake
  margin). The sign is fixed before scoring, not chosen by whichever orientation gives the
  higher AUROC. No training.
- *Block.* s_i = sigma(w^T phi(x_i) + b), the positive-class probability of a logistic
  regression (liblinear, class_weight balanced, max_iter 1000, seed 20260531), where x_i
  is the block's feature vector and phi is a median-impute + standardize pipeline. Weights
  w are fit on the training episodes only; the s_i used for scoring are the held-out
  test-split probabilities. The split is episode-level 70/30 stratified by class (seed
  20260531), with train/test episode sets asserted disjoint.

**AUROC over those scores.** Separability is measured by AUROC (Area Under the ROC Curve,
ROC = Receiver Operating Characteristic): the area under the true-positive vs
false-positive curve as the threshold on s sweeps all values. Equivalently, with positive
scores {s_i^+} (n_+ of them) and negative scores {s_j^-} (n_- of them),

    AUROC = (1 / (n_+ * n_-)) * sum_{i,j} [ 1(s_i^+ > s_j^-) + 0.5 * 1(s_i^+ = s_j^-) ]

the fraction of positive-negative pairs in which the positive scores higher (ties 0.5),
i.e. the probability a random positive is scored more failure-like than a random negative.
0.5 is chance, 1.0 perfect; it is threshold-free and invariant to monotone rescaling of s.

**Decision criterion.** The verdict for this axis is not raw AUROC but incremental value of
a block over the deployed-h (V_S) baseline: delta = AUROC(block) - AUROC(V_S-only) at the
same K. The baseline V_S-only uses deployed h only, because target-net h is absent from the
NPZ.

**Provenance.** The original pilot was computed inline and its code was not saved. The
numbers below are from the reproducible, committed script
`scripts/analysis/failure_signal_pilot.py` (deterministic; rerun is hash-identical;
sklearn AUROC cross-checked against the pairwise form above to 1e-9). A cell-by-cell
reconciliation against the original inline report found 20 cells differing by > 0.02; all
traced to two defects in the original that the fixed script removes: (i) single-signal
orientation chosen by best-AUROC rather than a fixed sign map, and (ii) unrecoverable
block feature/score choices. The high-level conclusions below survive the reconciliation;
the affected numbers are corrected to the reproducible values.

### 3.2 Collision — captured by V_S alone

Deployed-h single-signal AUROC is near-perfect at short lead (K=1 1.000, K=3 0.998) and
decays with horizon (mean 0.867; K=15 0.735, K=30 0.684). No block adds robust value over
deployed-h: mean incremental delta is +0.013 for h+h_dot, -0.003 for geometry, -0.005 for
action/filter, -0.048 for CBF-row, and -0.045 for all-signals. With only 25-26 collision
positives the per-lead deltas swing widely (e.g. all-signals K=30 delta -0.137), which is
small-sample overfitting, not signal: adding features to predict collision tends to hurt
on this few-positive set. Collision is highly predictable, but the prediction is already
carried by V_S; no additional signal is worth persisting for it.

### 3.3 Stuck — a signal beyond V_S exists, from CBF-row and geometry

Single-signal AUROC (mean over leads): deployed h 0.932, clearance 0.921, brake margin
0.889, projection 0.879, saturation 0.866. The all-signals block beats deployed-h at every
lead, by +0.010 to +0.042 (mean +0.026), stable across all six leads (105 positives). The
increment comes from the CBF-row (mean +0.015) and geometry (mean +0.017) blocks; the
action/filter block alone adds essentially nothing (mean -0.002). So a signal beyond V_S
exists for stuck, but it is carried by the CBF-row and geometry features, not by
projection/saturation on their own.

This corrects the earlier readiness-probe reading that stuck would be carried by
action/filter signals. Under exact-K labeling with a fixed sign map, h, clearance, and
brake margin are the strongest single separators; saturation and projection are weaker and
add little once h is present. Notably, closing speed is not a stuck signal at all
(single-signal AUROC 0.420, i.e. lower closing speed is mildly failure-like, the opposite
of collision) — the original inline report had it at 0.580 only because it flipped the
sign post hoc.

### 3.4 Lead time for stuck

Stuck has no saved onset field analogous to collision's box-empty / brake-margin onset.
Using AUROC-vs-K as a proxy, the top stuck signals first clear 0.7 and 0.8 only at K=30,
consistent with gradual entrapment rather than a sharp onset. No stuck onset label exists
in the artifacts.

## 4. Synthesis

| Failure | Share (N=2500) | Mechanism | Reactive-control solvable? | Predictable? | Originating layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collision | 26 (1.0%) | Box-infeasible filter myopia; 92.3% upstream-fixable, 0% observation-limit; lead 11-29 steps | Single-step lever insufficient; lookahead-alpha falsified (§1) | Yes, but fully captured by V_S; no incremental signal | Control / policy (upstream) |
| Stuck | 105 (4.2%) | Near-obstacle projection/saturation chatter in local-minimum geometry; 90.5% planning-limit | No — reactive control cannot escape the local minimum | Yes, with a measurable signal beyond V_S (all-signals mean +0.026, stable across leads), carried by CBF-row and geometry features | Planning (geometric) |
| Timeout | 27 (1.1%) | Not separately dissected | Not assessed | Not analyzed | Not analyzed |

The two dominant modes differ in kind. Collision is rare, perception-clean, and already
predicted by V_S; the one reactive lever tried (lookahead-alpha) backfired. Stuck is the
larger failure, is not solvable inside the reactive frame, and is predictable with a
signal that exceeds V_S.

## 6. Limitations

- The signal-pilot AUROCs are biased-sample existence checks, not operational detector
  metrics; the V_S baseline omits target-net h (absent from the NPZ), so the +0.026 stuck
  lift is measured against deployed-h only.
- The original pilot computation was not persisted; it was reimplemented as a committed,
  deterministic script and the numbers corrected. Reconciliation showed the original inline
  run had two defects (single-signal orientation chosen by best-AUROC, and unrecoverable
  block feature/score choices); the corrected reproducible values are used throughout this
  document.
- Failure dissection is single-seed (seed-42 checkpoint, diagnostic seed 45678); stuck
  separability is established on this sample only.
- Stuck has no onset label, so its lead time is characterized only by an AUROC-vs-K proxy.
- Timeout failures were not separately characterized.
