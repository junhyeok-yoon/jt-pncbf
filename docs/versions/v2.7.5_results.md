# v2.7.5 — RESULTS. Deploy control-rate axis (dt_ctrl 20 Hz -> 100 Hz), eval-only.

**Status: EXPLORATORY.** Single seed (42), eval-only on the v2.7.4 adopted checkpoint, no retraining, no
method change. Deploy-rate rows are `eval_only` and are NOT SOTA-bolded on cps alone (06_workflow §2.4);
their classification is the Researcher's. Every claim inherits the single-seed caveat.

## 1. What was tested

Whether raising the DEPLOY control period from 20 Hz to 100 Hz on an already-trained policy buys real
performance, and whether it buys it at the cost of bang-bang commands that would transfer badly under
motor lag. Three arms isolate the control rate from the integration grid:

| arm | dt_sim | dt_ctrl | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.05 | 0.05 | standing comparator and harness-reproduction gate |
| B | 0.01 | 0.05 | fine integration, 20 Hz control — isolates the integration grid |
| C | 0.01 | 0.01 | 100 Hz control — the axis under test |

B-vs-C is the control-rate effect. A-vs-B is the integration-grid effect and is reported separately,
never merged into the headline.

**Frozen inputs.** Checkpoint `data/runs/v2.7.4/set__20260720-083533__seed42/
v2.7.4__20260720-091830__seed42/checkpoints/best.pt` (jt_pncbf, step 39000, SHA-256 `244f4f83`);
pool `eval_full_quadrotor-3d-d2r_n2000_seed23456` (n=2000, manifest SHA-256 `0ef3751b`); eval batch 2000
(05_code §7 pin, identical across arms); alpha 2.0/100.0; `filter.empty_fallback.mode = none`; h_star,
observation, policy, filter unchanged. Physical episode duration 10.00 s and stuck window 3.00 s
(r_stuck 0.10 m) held across all arms — at dt=0.01 that is max_steps 1000 and stuck window 300 steps,
verified from each arm's written config. Step-denominated parameters were rescaled; seconds were held.

**Gate passed.** Arm A reproduced the registered v2.7.4 cps exactly: 0.850837 vs 0.8508367, delta 0.0.
This also establishes device consistency — the registered v2.7.4 number came from the same CPU eval path.

## 4. Diagnostic analysis (M4-M5) — POST-HOC, verdicts unchanged

All 17 pre-declared tests in `m5_analysis_plan.json` are reported, nulls included. The plan was written
before any of them was computed. All three arms passed the 1e-6 regeneration gate against their
registered metrics (B 4.21e-7, C 4.56e-7), so the per-episode data underlying this section is the
recorded data, not a subset re-run.

### 4.1 The net is a small residual of large bidirectional churn — the version's main structural finding

118 of 2000 outcomes changed. Decomposing the full 5x5 paired outcome table by cps weight: gross positive
movement +139.5 cps-units, gross negative -81.5, **net +58.0**. The signal is **26% of the total
movement**. The table independently reconstructs the registered delta (+0.032420 predicted vs +0.032396
observed, residual 2.4e-5 from rounding), so this decomposition is not an approximation.

Collision flips (24 forward, 12 reverse) are under a third of the churn. The rest is timeout->goal 23 and
oob->goal 13 against goal->timeout 19 and goal->oob 8.

Consequence: **100 Hz is not a gentle refinement but a substantial reshuffling of closed-loop behaviour
whose net is modestly positive.** A net that is the residual of large bidirectional movement is fragile
to seed and pool. Any headline claim on control rate requires multi-seed; single-seed is not sufficient.

Median per-episode cps is essentially unchanged (B 0.99231, C 0.99543): the body of the distribution does
not move, only the tail.

### 4.2 The improvement is net, never universal

24 forward flips (B collides, C reaches) against 12 reverse (C collides, B reaches). Reverse flips are
named, never absorbed into an aggregate. Discordant-pair tests straddle 0.05 and disagree with each
other — exact one-sided 0.0326, exact two-sided 0.0652, McNemar without continuity correction 0.0455,
with correction 0.0668. **That disagreement is the finding; no significance claim is made in either
direction.**

### 4.3 Paired testing separates where the marginal test could not

Paired per-scene difference, bootstrap 95% CI: **C-B [+0.0101, +0.0549]** excludes zero;
**B-A [-0.0257, +0.0196]** includes zero.

Two consequences. First, the grid-is-noise reading survives pairing, so attributing the B-vs-C difference
to control rate is not qualified. Second, and more general: per-scene cps on this pool is bimodal (most
scenes near 0.99, failures near -2.3), so marginal CI width is dominated by scene-difficulty variance
that cancels within a pair. **On a bit-identical pool the marginal CI is structurally underpowered.**
This is recorded as an observed property of same-pool comparisons on this metric; it is not adopted as a
registration rule.

H1 remains REJECTED on its registered marginal criterion. The paired result does not revise it.

### 4.4 The roughness is filter-induced and localized — the deployment-critical result

The TV/s increase survives both duration controls, so it is not an artifact of short episodes:

| measure | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|
| registered (full pool) | +40.5% | +95.5% |
| restricted to scenes both arms reached | +36.4% | +91.2% |
| common window [0, min(T_B, T_C)] | +47.2% | +98.0% |

Split by filter state: **u_cmd TV/s in filter-ACTIVE intervals +121% (5.23 -> 11.55, box-normalized);
filter-INACTIVE -13%; u_nom (policy) TV/s -10.8%.**

Mechanism: the policy is a feedback map and gets smoother as dt falls, while the HardNet path is
branch-discrete (box-aware argmin plus the filter's own activation switch), so its discrete transitions
occur five times as often. The roughness is entirely the filter's, not the policy's.

Deployment consequence: typical motor time constants (20-50 ms) low-pass exactly this band, so the thrust
actually applied would diverge from `u_cmd` and the premise of the CBF condition — that the commanded
input is applied — degrades. This is the single strongest reason not to adopt 100 Hz before the actuator-
lag axis is run.

### 4.5 What was NOT established — nulls, recorded as such

- **Infeasibility is not the mechanism of the reverse flips.** With the forward flips as a control, the
  arm that collides has the higher infeasibility in BOTH directions (reverse: B 0.110 -> C 0.227; forward:
  B 0.327 -> C 0.120), and empty-before-divergence ordering is essentially the same in both groups
  (75% vs 67%). Infeasibility tracks collision rather than control rate. An earlier reading that the
  reverse flips gave the deferred per-system `empty_fallback` plumbing a concrete motivation is
  **withdrawn**; that work remains justified by the global-config defect alone.
- **No new boundary-riding failure mode was detected.** The pre-declared detector fired on 0 scenes in B
  and 0 in C; thresholds were not loosened after the null. Stuck rose 0.0000 -> 0.0005, one scene of 2000,
  which cannot support a new-failure-mode claim. Note the scope: this is a null about the detector **as
  operationally defined**, not a demonstration that no such behaviour exists — scene 291 shows C
  sustaining full-box alternation with h_star near zero for roughly 6 s and ending stuck while B kept
  moving and timed out. Whether the definition or the visual reading was at fault is unresolved; the
  cheap check is to record what scene 291 actually attained on each declared threshold.
- **Initial tilt and initial speed separate nothing.** Unlike v2.7.4, where tilt was the stratification
  axis, the control-rate effect is not tilt-mediated.

### 4.6 Where the effect lives

The only separating covariate is path-blocked: forward flips 0.917 [0.792, 1.000], reverse 0.750
[0.500, 1.000], no-flip 0.414 [0.393, 0.436]. Flips of either direction concentrate on blocked scenes,
consistent with 4.4 — control rate matters where the geometry forces the filter active. The reverse-flip
CI is wide (n=12); forward and reverse cannot be distinguished on this covariate. Exploratory subgroup
analysis, single seed.

### 4.7 The descent residual persists

Both flip groups are predominantly descent collisions — the same residual v2.7.4 localized, where
altitude bled during recovery and an obstacle was reached before recovery completed. v2.7.4 established
that recovery speed was not the lever; v2.7.5 adds that control rate is not either. Two independent axes
have now failed to move it.

## 5. Code changes carried by this version

1. **`eval.dt_ctrl` decoupling** (`src/eval/rollout.py`, eval path only): control recomputed once per
   `substeps = round(dt_ctrl/dt_sim)` and held (ZOH) across substeps, with filter flags captured at
   compute time and held. Integer-multiple validated. Default `dt_ctrl = dt_sim` gives substeps = 1 and
   is bit-parity with pre-v2.7.5. `rollout()` and all training paths untouched.
2. **Graph-retention fix** (same file): the eval rollout retained an autograd graph per control
   computation — `u_nom = policy(obs(x))` graphs back to `x`, so `x_{t+1} = rk4(x_t, u_safe)` accumulated
   every step — which OOM-killed Arm C at 27.7 GiB RSS on a 31 GiB host (`global_oom`, journalctl). Fixed
   by detaching the state carried forward and the recorded tensors; no blanket `no_grad`, which would
   have killed the grad-h computation in `_cbf_terms`. Proven inert (Arm A bit-identical, delta 0.0) and
   proven to remove rather than reduce the leak: at fixed step count and storage, peak RSS 1956.6 MiB at
   200 control computations vs 1962.9 MiB at 1000, ratio 1.003 against a 5x computation ratio.

`scripts/verify.sh`: 175 passed / 2 skipped / 0 failed after both changes.

## 6. Deployment recommendation

**Keep `dt_ctrl` at 0.05 (20 Hz) as the deployed default.** The flag stays available. Rationale: the net
gain is modest and CI-overlapping on the registered test; it is single-seed; it is the 26% residual of
large bidirectional churn; and it comes with +121% commanded total variation inside filter-active
intervals, which is precisely what actuator lag would punish. Re-evaluate after the lag axis.
Classification of these rows is the Researcher's (06_workflow §2.4).

## 7. Next axes this version points to

- **Actuator lag** (`src/common/motor_lag.py`, implemented, off by default). Whether the 100 Hz gain
  survives lag is the operative test for adopting the rate at all. Adding it forces the observation to
  dim 36 so V_hat sees the rotor state.
- **Altitude retention during recovery** for the descent residual, now supported by two independent axes
  failing to move it.
- **Multi-seed** before any control-rate claim is stated as a result rather than an observation.
