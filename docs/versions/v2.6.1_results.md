## 4. Next axis (the version's real output)

A diagnostic suite located both residual drains in the OBJECTIVE, not the certificate or the filter:

- **The certificate performs its role; the policy defers safety to the filter.** V̂ is sound (99.2%
  certified-truly-safe, 0/159 collision mis-certifications, strong B_0 authority) — never the collision
  culprit. But the filter rescues 64.4% of the policy's standalone failures, and 83% of residual collisions
  have the nominal accelerating toward the obstacle (role_function_diag). cps is substantially propped up by
  the filter, not a self-sufficient policy — the JT-viability concern.
- **Both failures are situation-dependent speed control the objective never taught.** Timeout: 57% of
  timeouts reach the goal radius but do not settle (goal-distance is quadratic ‖p-g‖^2, whose gradient
  vanishes near the goal; settling lives only in the sparse terminal — task_cost_surface). Fast-approach
  collision: the policy enters obstacles at high inward velocity, so L_f V̂ exceeds the box authority and the
  filter is infeasible (100% of collisions; a larger box cannot fix an unbounded L_f V̂). Both are the
  running objective's flat/absent situation-dependent velocity credit.
- **Refuted / rejected levers (do not revisit):** predictive-filter lookahead (alpha-scaling, null across a
  9-cell sweep — D2 fast-approach is entered before the window); a separate goal/reach-value NN (refuted on
  JT-native grounds: as a BPTT soft term it is actor-critic, as a second filter constraint it absorbs the
  policy's navigation role — either way a graft, not a JT-native extension); further torque enlargement
  (already plant-coherent); control-smoothness (already FAILED across forms).

**Direction (v2.6.2): a situation-dependent velocity objective**, inside policy_bptt_loss only (no value /
buffer / filter / structure change — the JT center is preserved). Non-vanishing goal-distance credit
(Huber), a goal-proximity settling term (dense, replacing the sparse terminal's role), and — as a candidate
second component — an obstacle-approach-speed penalty targeting the fast-approach collisions. Whether goal
(timeout) and obstacle (collision) components ship together with an ablation or split across versions is the
opening decision. The objective's ceiling then determines where novelty lives: if the objective reaches
~0.9, JT-with-a-better-objective is the contribution; if it falls short, the gap grounds a certificate
redefinition (a viability-consistent V̂ or a single reach-avoid PNCBF) — a theory-first question, not a
graft. The feasibility (sec:feasibility) and liveness (sec:liveness) theory additions authored this version
frame both the collision-feasibility bound and this objective-vs-certificate fork.
