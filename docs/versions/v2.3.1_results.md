# v2.3.1 — results

System: double_integrator. Axis: fast-approach coverage — raise `v_init_max` 0.5→2.0 with a
low-speed-weighted magnitude+angle start-speed PDF, plus the admission unit-fix (B). Single training
axis on the v2.3.0 SOTA-standard config (D_pi=2k, D_V=1M, injection OFF, gsr 0.30), seed 42, 50k.
Eval standard: full n2000 / seed 23456 @ goal_speed_radius 0.30, n2000 re-selection over the top-8
in-loop checkpoints. Single-seed (flagged). SOTA of record entering v2.3.1: DI cps 0.8698 (v2.3.0
3-seed mean; seed-42 0.8700), n2000 @0.30.

## Part I — what was tested

Two changes shipped together as one intended intervention:
- **B (admission unit-fix, prerequisite):** `_passes_unavoidable_collision_filter` inward term changed
  from a projected *distance* `dot(unit(v), center−start)` to the projected *speed*
  `dot(v_m/s, unit(center−start))`, matching the `v²/(2a)` braking criterion (03_train §1.3 intent).
  Parity-confirmed inert at v_init_max=0.5 (0 flips on both secured pools), so it does not affect the
  v2.3.0 baseline behavior.
- **A (start-speed distribution, the axis):** DI initial velocity sampled as magnitude+angle —
  low-speed-weighted linear speed PDF `f(s)=1.3/V−(0.6/V²)s` on `[0,V]` (V=v_init_max) + uniform angle —
  with `v_init_max` 0.5→2.0. Motivation: the prior collision-speed analysis (v2.2.1) showed collisions
  occur at speeds the policy builds up mid-rollout (impact v_close median 1.28, only ~10% < 0.5), which
  the v_init_max=0.5 starts never sample; raising v_init_max was hypothesized to put fast-approach
  states into collection and improve V_S feasibility there.

Eval pools were kept FROZEN (loaded, never rebuilt) for comparison continuity vs the 0.5 SOTA.

| v2.3.0 seed-42 (0.8700) | 0.8700 | [0.8488, 0.8888] | 0.9470 | 0.0075 | 0.0875 |
| Δ | **−0.0277** | (CI-overlap) | −0.009 | −0.0005 | **+0.0273** |

(best.pt @45000 alternative: cps 0.8312, infeas 0.1395.) vs SOTA 0.8568: −0.0145, CI includes.

n2000; the cost is higher infeasibility.

## Part III — in-distribution diagnostic (decisive)

To test whether the regression was merely train/eval distribution mismatch, a temporary n2000 pool was
drawn at the v2.3.1 training distribution (v_init_max=2.0, magnitude+angle sampler, seed 34567, temp dir;
secured pools left byte-identical) and BOTH policies were scored on that same fast-start pool:

| policy (temp 2.0 pool, @0.30) | cps | 95% CI | reach | collision | infeas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2.3.1 @45000 (in-loop best) | 0.8566 | [0.8366, 0.8767] | 0.948 | 0.0060 | 0.1337 |
| v2.3.1 @40500 (0.5-reselect best) | 0.8559 | [0.8371, 0.8749] | 0.942 | 0.0035 | 0.1154 |
| **v2.3.0 @40500 (SOTA seed-42)** | **0.8927** | [0.8744, 0.9107] | 0.961 | 0.0085 | **0.0911** |

**On v2.3.1's OWN (fast) training distribution, v2.3.0 still wins by ~0.036 cps and has LOWER
infeasibility (0.091 vs 0.115–0.134)** — v2.3.0's point sits above v2.3.1's CI upper
(near-non-overlapping). Neither cps is comparable to the 0.8698 SOTA (different pool); this is a mechanism
diagnostic only.

## Part IV — improvement verdict

**REGRESSED. SOTA UNCHANGED at 0.8698.** Single-seed; on the frozen benchmark the CI overlaps both v2.3.0
and SOTA, so by 04_eval §5 this is "no improvement detected," and the point estimate is −0.028.

**Versus the hypothesis — REFUTED, and refuted in-distribution.** The premise (fast-approach coverage →
better feasibility on fast-approach states) fails on its own terms: the v_init_max=2.0 policy has *higher*
infeasibility than the 0.5-trained SOTA even on a 2.0 pool. So the regression is the **policy itself, not an
eval-distribution mismatch** — raising v_init_max produced a strictly worse value/policy (consistent with
the high infeas-volatility observed throughout training). B (the admission unit-fix) is an orthogonal
correctness improvement, parity-inert at 0.5, and is kept.

## Part V — next axis (suggestion only)

Raising `v_init_max` is a **dead lever** for this benchmark: it does not improve — it degrades — value
feasibility, including in-distribution. The DI cps cap remains what the v2.3.0 collection audit identified:
**projection infeasibility on recoverable (small-‖L_g h‖ / near-boundary) states**, which neither buffer
recency (v2.3.0) nor start-speed coverage (v2.3.1) moved, and which the v2.2.1 feasibility-shaping loss
(L_feas) addressed only partially before being set aside. That cap is still open. No specific next
experiment is proposed here.
